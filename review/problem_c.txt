             Problem C The Problem of Random Parking of Shared Bicycles

      Since their launch, shared bicycles have gained great popularity among users. In
many cities, they have become residents' preferred travel option besides public
transport, substantially easing the pressure on urban road networks and reducing
traffic congestion. As the concepts of green travel and environmental protection take
deeper root among the public, more users will choose shared bicycles, further
fulfilling the goal of bringing bicycles back to cities. Nevertheless, lagging
management has led to prominent problems such as chaotic parking, severe vandalism
and disordered spatial distribution.

      The appendix records the usage data of more than 400,000 Mobike bicycles by
over 300,000 Mobike users in a city over two weeks in May 2017. This open-source
dataset comes from the 2017 Mobike Algorithm Challenge. It contains more than 3
million travel records. Each record consists of the order ID (`orderid`), user ID
(`userid`), bicycle ID (`bikeid`), bicycle type (`biketype`), start time of use
(`starttime`), starting coordinate (`startloc`) and ending coordinate (`endloc`). The
starting and ending coordinates are encrypted with geohash; team members can
retrieve latitude and longitude through open-source methods. Please make full use of
this dataset together with supplementary necessary data, and adopt mathematical
modeling to solve the following problems:

      (1) Establish a mathematical model for users' bicycle travel behavior based on
the data.

      (2) Spatial-temporal imbalance of bicycle distribution is a major challenge for
shared-bicycle systems. Shared-bicycle platforms deploy trucks, tricycles and other
vehicles to reposition bicycles to mitigate this issue. Based on the travel behavior
model built above, propose an optimal bicycle rebalancing and scheduling plan for a
typical day in this city.

      (3) Some researchers propose incentivizing users to ride bicycles to locations
desired by the platform, so as to partially or fully alleviate the above problem. Explore
and present your user incentive scheme.

      (4) Another major issue is unrestricted parking, which causes traffic jams and
undermines urban appearance. Electronic fencing has become the primary technical
solution, and many cities in China have partially rolled it out. Using the travel
behaviour model from Question (1) to predict parking demand at different locations,
provide suggestions on the layout and size planning of electronic fences in this city.

      (5) Discuss how factors such as the coexistence of multiple competing
shared-bicycle platforms affect the resolution of the above problems.
